Summary

TRAPPIST-1’s seven planets are all close to Earth’s size but receive very different amounts of starlight. The innermost planets get several times Earth’s irradiation; the outer ones far less. Size and irradiation alone cannot tell us whether any of them holds liquid water.

1. Data and method

Values come from the NASA Exoplanet Archive composite table pscomppars, fetched live at 2026-10-25 15:55 UTC. The comparison plots planet radius against stellar irradiation for 7 planets; 0 were excluded for missing values. A related Sample used the Interpretation “small” → radius < 2 R⊕ (a size threshold chosen for this search, not a scientific category), matching 1,228 planets with 37 excluded for missing values.

2. Results

Planet e receives about two-thirds of Earth’s irradiation and is slightly smaller than Earth, which is why it is often discussed as a temperate world. Planets b and c receive far more energy than Earth.

3. Limitations

These values combine several publications and may not be mutually consistent. Equilibrium temperature is not a measured surface temperature, and radius and irradiation do not establish an atmosphere, oceans or habitability.

Template note: pscomppars mixes values from different papers (radius and irradiation: Agol et al. 2021; equilibrium temperature: Ducrot et al. 2020; orbital shape: Grimm et al. 2018).
